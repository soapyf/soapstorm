# Atmo Magic: the ground strike - aura, amber, plasma, crawl, sparks, fire

Rationale for the 2026-09 rework of `sslightning.*`, `sslightningrender.*` and `ssLightning{V,F}.glsl`. The code carries one-line comments; the why lives here. Companion: `doc/archive/atmo_magic_lightning.md` (the original design, still right about scheduling, thunder, channel morphology and the compositing order), `doc/viewer/glow_and_alpha.md` (screen alpha is the bloom mask in this pass).

## The reference

A 25 fps dashcam clip of a bolt hitting a moving car on a wet road, frames 2-12 (t = 0 at frame 2):

| t | column | ground |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | wide blazing white core, pale blue-violet sheath, beaded; scene washed pale blue | over-exposed orange-white flare, 6-10 m wide, wider than tall |
| 0.16 | a beaded white-cyan plasma column ~2x the bolt's width, soft, upper part still white | a 15-20 m line of yellow-orange fire; the whole frame, sky and rain, washed orange |
| 0.24 | thinner, wisps and vortices; upper white, lower part cyan-green | a chain of discrete orange blobs along the road, reflected in the wet surface |
| 0.28-0.32 | curling wisps; the lowest knot greenish-yellow-white, several times brighter than the wisps | orange blobs, dimming |
| 0.36 | RESTRIKE: a chain of distinct beads down the same column, old wisps under it | fire still burning |
| 0.40 | faint grey wisps; the yellow knot still bright | dim orange, still there |

What that fixes in the model: the column's plasma phase lasts ~0.4 s and goes white-blue, white-cyan, grey while widening and breaking into curling wisps; only the foot goes cyan-green, yellow-green, then a yellow knot that outlives and outshines the wisps; the ground fire is amber, lateral (a line of blobs, not a spot), ignites over two or three frames and outlasts the column; a restrike can come 0.3-0.4 s later, down the same channel, as a beaded chain; the orange wash is scene-wide, so it is a light plus a veil, not a sprite.

## Two invariants kept

Strike geometry is decided at spawn, ten seconds early, from streams seeded by the fire time - the crawl, the spark table, the fire blobs, the aura discs, the per-node tip distances and pop thresholds are all built once. Per-frame work is closed-form evaluation of hashes and envelopes; nothing simulates state. The renderer stays stateless: every envelope it reads (`mChargeHeld`, `mHit`, `mFire`, `mPlasmaSince`, `mLastGap`, the per-stroke scale and drift) is computed in `SSLightning::advance()`.

## The vertex buffer (why immediate mode had to go)

`gGL` carries position, one texcoord and an 8-bit colour. Every ask below needs more per vertex: an amber weight that interpolates across a 100 m far-LOD segment, a plasma phase per node, an HDR brightness above white (8-bit colour clamps at 1.0, which is why the old `AMBER_BOOST` never showed), the strike point in a disc's own frame, an anchor depth. So the pass builds one `LLVertexBuffer` per frame (`MAP_VERTEX|NORMAL|TANGENT|TEXCOORD0|TEXCOORD1|COLOR`, U32 indices, the precip renderer's pattern) and draws it in one call; additive blending is order-independent so ribbons, sparks and discs share the buffer. The flash discs and the debug markers go through the same buffer, because a shader that declares `normal`/`tangent` makes the immediate-mode path read recycled memory for them.

Per vertex: `normal` = ribbon (strike seed, amber weight, dissolve age) / disc (anchor depth, height above surface in radii); `tangent` = (HDR brightness, plasma noise LOD, dissolve flow amplitude or depth soft width, mode); `texcoord1` = ribbon (bead multiplier, world up along the strip); colour = tint plus the bloom FRACTION in alpha. Modes: 0 live core and its dissolving plasma copy, 1 sheath, 2 aura/flare/fire disc (the fraction of the mode carries the flare's share), 3 plain ribbon (sparks), 4 sky flash disc (the old cubic profile and alpha rule, plus a 30-60m camera pull with the radius rescaled for exact parallax and a ground fade off aux.y - at true depth the lowest trunk disc cut a hard chord across the landscape; the discs are placed only on trunk the leader has reached, because pre-lighting the whole trunk glowed the impact point ~leader_s early), 5 flat fill (wash, markers), 6 occlusion box (writes zero, never discards), 7 steam puff (alpha-blended, its own batch).

Fragment output everywhere: `rgb = min(tint * bright * mask, 3)`, `alpha = glow * bloom_fraction * mask * min(bright, 1)`. Brightness above white reaches the tonemapper through the RGBA16F screen target; the bloom seed is never inflated by it (rule 3 of glow_and_alpha).

**The mode must be read with an epsilon.** `ctl.w` holds one constant across a quad's four vertices, but perspective-correct interpolation of a constant is only exact in exact arithmetic: a 5.0 arrives as 4.9999995 on some pixels and 5.0000005 on others. A bare `int(floor(ctl.w))` therefore draws every mode as itself on roughly half its pixels and as the mode BELOW it on the rest - a marker's flat fill as a flash disc, a flash disc as a plain ribbon complete with its hard quad edge, an aura disc as a sheath, the sheath as the beaded core. It reads as a per-pixel stipple over solid quads, and it is easy to mistake for a noise or vertex-buffer fault; the tell is that the live core is untouched, because its 0 floors to -1 and falls through to the same ribbon path anyway. `floor(ctl.w + 0.001)` fixes the dispatch, and mode 2's flare share must then be taken as `ctl.w - float(mode)` rather than `fract(ctl.w)` - on a flare-less disc whose 2.0 landed at 1.9999999, `fract` returns 0.9999999 and hands it the full contact spike.

## The asks, one by one

**Sparks land and spread.** The old roll coupled vertical to horizontal speed (`speed x rise`), so most sparks flew 2-5 s against a 1.4 s life and never landed - and the strike retired at ~1 s anyway, cutting them off mid-air. Now each spark is a spawn-time seed: flight time 0.28-0.95 s (vertical launch derived from it, so every one lands), landing distance 3-28 m x (0.6 + 0.4 intensity) x `SparkSpread`, heading fanned along the crawl for 60 % of them and 40 % launched from crawl nodes, the surface crossing solved once (`surfaceZ`), a roof edge or wall (more than 2.5 m of drop or rise) letting the spark fly off. Colour walks white, orange, red; the head widens to a 1.2-pixel floor at range with its brightness handed back. Retirement now waits for the longest spark plus its secondaries. A third of the fan leaves steep - risers with 2.2-4x the vertical launch and less than half the ground speed, living most of their own taller arc - because the flat ballistic roll alone sent every spark skimming to the ground and nothing visibly climbed.

**Charge aura.** Old: four discs re-hashed per frame from a spread that CONTRACTED as the charge rose (the haze grew, then shrank to half a metre), gated on at 20 % charge with a squared envelope and a cubed pulse (a pop), no depth fade (a hard chord through the road). New: five discs fixed at spawn (four patches on a 1.5-4 m ring plus the centre), radius (1.2 + intensity) x (0.7-1.3) m near, growing 30 % to 100 % over the first 60 % of the charge, arriving one by one, envelope `smoothstep(0, 0.35, held) * held^1.5` from exactly zero, pulse with a 0.45 floor. Held through the leader (`mChargeHeld`; `mCharge` itself snaps to zero at leader start, up to 1.2 s before contact) and decayed over 60 ms after contact under the flare.

Subtlety is calibrated by light, not by peak: `AURA_GAIN` 0.032 at dial 1 lands the time-integrated light at roughly a third of the old corona's (larger discs and a pulse floor against a tenth of the old peak); dial 3 is about the old amount of light, 10 the old peak. The dial goes to 10 for that reason.

The aura ships OFF (`Aura` defaults to 0): the violet charge blobs read as noise over most storms, and the amber contact flare - which reuses the same discs but rides its own `HitFlare` dial - carries the ground-strike glow on its own. The hollow (dimming each disc toward the strike point) was removed outright once the aura went dark by default; it never read at all, and it took a per-vertex uv frame and a shader branch with it.

Clipping, three layers: (1) the pull - each disc is moved toward the camera along the live view ray by `min(clamp(0.12 d, 4, 120) * AuraPull, 0.5 d)` and its radius rescaled by the same factor, so it projects exactly where its true point does (parallax by construction) while its depth beats kerbs, cars and terrain lips; (2) a per-vertex height fade against the cached surface height, which is what removes the hard chord at grazing views where a metre of depth fade is a tenth of a pixel; (3) with a depth copy, an anchor compare (fade where the scene sits in front of ~55-70 % of the strike point's depth - lenient, because at a grazing view the road under the disc is nearer than the point without hiding it) and the puff-style soft fade against the disc's own drawn depth. The pull deliberately draws the disc over occluders within the pull distance - that is the "covers the location visually" ask.

**Amber gradient.** Old: computed per SEGMENT from the segment midpoint's height against a constant 18 m zone, applied flat to the whole quad - with 12-100 m segments at most one fell in the zone, a step on one quad, sub-pixel at a kilometre. New: `finishChannel` walks from the trunk foot up the parents accumulating path metres (`mTipDistM`), branches inherit their fork's figure, the crawl is zero; the renderer interpolates the weight per vertex. Zone = `AmberZone` (25 m) x (1 + 2 x ground distance / 1 km), capped at 2.5 km's worth, never under forty pixels, positive bolts 1.3x. Core tint mixes toward `AMBER_CORE` then `AMBER_HOT` in the lowest 12 %, sheath toward `AMBER_GLOW`, width flares 1 + 0.9 w, brightness 1 + 2.2 w^2 above white through the float attribute. Ground strikes pivot about the foot: the wind drift between strokes is weighted by tip distance so the attachment and crawl never move.

**Amber reuses the aura on hit.** Same five discs; each disc's colour is the SUM of the violet term and the amber term (`FLARE_MID x HitFlare x mHit x (0.5 patch / 1.0 centre)`), normalised so the tint stays 8-bit and the excess rides brightness, so the crossfade needs no palette switch and no pop. The flare's share `q` selects a power-law spike `1.6 q (1-r)^6` at the contact - the blazing point inside the wide glow of frame 2 - blending to the over-exposed `HOT_COLOR`. `mHit` sums fired strokes at 1.5x the stroke decay with a 20 ms square-root rise, so it fades with the bolt one decay behind it and every restrike re-lights it. The centre disc is a 1.6:0.8 ellipse (world-horizontal), radius 2 + 2 x intensity m. The flare lands the instant the leader visibly connects, because the leader clock is normalised by the TRUNK's length (see `finishChannel`): normalising by the channel's longest chain - which the crawl and branch chains stretch - had the foot touching down up to half a second before `mT 0`, with the flare and return stroke reading as late.

**Plasma.** Two layers with two deaths, and keeping them separate is what makes restrikes work. The WIDE GLOW - core, sheath, the amber fan at the foot - only ever FADES, on its own stroke's decay; it is never popped or masked. The THIN PLASMA copy - the same ribbon geometry at the bare channel width, no growth, no amber widening - is what DISSOLVES: per-node spatially coherent pop thresholds (25 m value noise plus jitter) schedule when each stretch's plasma phase starts, and from then the shader's animated mask eats it (the section below). Its brightness slides only ~30 % over the phase (per end, so the slower amber foot cools slower) - the mask is the death, not a fade. The earlier design also popped the core out from under the plasma, which shredded every restrike 50 ms in while still at full brightness; now a fresh stroke flashes as a solid bolt over the old wisps, and the pop survives only in the embers fallback (`Plasma` 0). The colour walks the recorded ramps: air white, white-cyan, grey; foot white-yellow, yellow-green, yellow knot with an HDR boost where the eroded plasma is densest. A distance LOD fades the fine mask octave as the core drops under three pixels. Plasma is drawn for the LATEST stroke only (plus the previous one when the gap exceeded 0.15 s, so grey wisps hang under a fresh bolt), never stacked per stroke. The live core beads too (`Bead`), and a late restrike multiplies the beading by how far the previous copy had cooled - the re-lit column is a chain of distinct beads. The cheap path (sheath, sparks, intact core with beading off) is one power and no hashes, so the dominant fill costs what it did.

**Ground crawl.** Grown in `buildChannel` right after the trunk, before any branches (the node cap cannot starve it), on its own stream: length 30 m x u^1.6 x `Crawl` (median ~9 m, a quarter under 1.5 m and skipped), lengthened by wetness at the foot, one arm or two (35 %, the second at 40 % length), 1.5-3 m steps, three pre-rolled headings per step scored by the wetness two steps ahead (`CrawlWet`), the surface from `SSLightning::surfaceZ` (surface field, then the flowmap's height capture, then terrain, never below water - no raycasts, which walk the whole octree), stopping at a 2 m jump. Crawl nodes join `mChannel` as non-trunk nodes off the foot so amber, plasma, joints and the leader all work unchanged; the renderer draws them as GROUND-CONFORMING quads lifted 12 cm (a camera-faced card at ground level buries its lower half in the road with a hard chord), with a thinner sheath and a faster plasma so a gap opens under the knot as in the late frames. The attachment search gains ten ring probes so standing water on open ground (where the flowmap has no columns) can pull the strike: a full puddle is worth three metres of height.

**Ground fire.** A third element, because the frames show the ground glow outliving the column two to three times and neither the aura (which must fade with the bolt, by request) nor the sparks can do that. Blobs seeded at spawn: one every ~1.8 m along the crawl (up to 14), a fan of five within 1.5-5 m when the crawl is under 4 m (so the impact-time fire line always exists), and landing embers under half the landing sparks (up to 16). Each ignites `dist / 60 m/s` after a stroke (the fire runs outward along the crawl), rises over 80 ms, and burns `exp(-age / (tau_f x life_mul))` with `tau_f = GroundFireLife / 2.5`, flickering, cooling from `FIRE_0` through `FIRE_1` to `FIRE_2`. Crawl and fan blobs age from the latest stroke (a restrike re-ignites them); embers from their own touchdown. Drawn as mode-2 discs with the same pull and fades as the aura, a small spike, a 2-pixel floor, within 800 m.

**Lighting.** The bolt light is unchanged. A second amber point light per ground strike (`FIRE_LIGHT`, radius 25 + 35 x intensity + crawl length, at the foot-to-crawl midpoint, driven by `max(mFire, 0.5 mHit)` and `FireLight`) is pushed after the bolt lights so it is the first to drop at the four-light budget - it is what turns the wet road orange and puts the reflections in the water. The scene-wide orange (frames 6-8 are orange in the sky and the rain, which a point light cannot do) is a fullscreen additive veil drawn in `renderFlash` before the puffs: `AMBER_GLOW x 0.12 x (0.85 mFire + 0.15 mHit) x intensity x FireLight / (1 + (d/80)^2)`, no bloom seed (a fullscreen alpha would dip the exposure), so at the stroke instant the blue flash discs dominate and the orange arrives ~0.1 s later.

**Restrike.** The fast series is unchanged (30-90 ms negative, 15-40 ms positive). After it, two unconditional rolls: with `LateRestrike` odds (0.45; positive bolts 0.65x) one more stroke fires 0.25-0.50 s after the last, at 0.75 scale. Rolling after the loop leaves every earlier draw where it was. Per-stroke wind drift is accumulated as `min(gap, 0.09)` per fast gap and 0.03 for the late gap - a dart leader follows the existing channel and must not land metres downwind of it.

**The crawl merges its corners too.** The joint-merge pass that gives the airborne channel its shared corner edges skipped crawl nodes outright, so the crawl was the only strip in the pass drawn as loose butted segments and every change of direction showed either a notch or an overlap. It merges now, but in its OWN frame: both sides of a joint have to be built against the same reference or the averaged corner means nothing, so a crawl joint - two segments lying in the ground plane, sided against z - merges against z, while an air joint merges against the view as before. The joint BETWEEN the two frames, at the foot where the falling channel becomes the crawl, is deliberately left a plain butt; there is no single side vector those two quads share. `groundRibbon` takes the same optional side pair `ribbon` does, and a joint the pass declined to merge leaves a zero vector that reads as the butt it should be.

**Crawl over sculpted and mesh ground.** `SSSurfaceField::sample` was nearest-cell for every field it carries, height included, so the surface it described was a staircase. The crawl steps 1.5-3 m and refuses a step of more than `CRAWL_JUMP_M` (2 m), so it read each cell boundary as a cliff - invisible over the Linden heightmap, where neighbouring cells differ by centimetres, and fatal over a sculpted or mesh sim surround, where they differ by the whole relief and the arm died on its first step. Two changes: the height (only the height - wetness, snow and puddle stay nearest-cell, and the puddle mask has its own noise) is now bilinear over the four surrounding cell CENTRES, and the crawl applies its continuity guard per candidate heading rather than to the arm, so a step that would climb a wall is simply not bid and only a position boxed in on all three headings ends the arm. Both matter: interpolation alone still leaves genuine walls, and per-candidate rejection alone still staircases.

**The plasma is the channel dissolving, not a second object over it.** Same ribbon geometry, same glowing bolt; what changes is that an animated alpha mask takes it away while a flow map curls what is left. The fault in the first version was the WIDTH: it grew the strip from twice the core to six times it as the copy aged, and widening a strip with age is exactly how you draw a wave. It read as one - a front travelling out along the bolt with a void behind it, spread far wider than the reference, and never breaking into anything. The recorded frames show the opposite: the column stays where it stood and comes apart in place. The plasma copy is now the BARE channel width - no growth at all, and none of the amber foot's 1.9x fan either, which otherwise printed the foot's widening triangle as dissolving cloud; the blobs form on the thin core and stay there.

Two pieces do the work. The **flow map** is the physics: two noise channels read as a vector, the pair rolling so the curl is alive rather than a fixed distortion, plus a steady convection term lifting along whichever way world up runs in that strip's frame. That direction cannot be assumed - it is passed per vertex in `texcoord1.y` as `-normalize(seg).z`, +1 for a channel running straight down and 0 where it lies flat and there is nowhere along the strip to rise. The vector displaces the coordinate the mask is sampled at, and the displacement grows with age, so the column is barely disturbed early and curling and climbing late. It is kept deliberately small.

The **animated alpha mask** is what dissolves rather than fades: a threshold rising with age eats the noise field wherever it is thinnest, so the channel tears into small clumps that shrink and go out on their own, and the hot middle survives longest because the profile weights it. A plain alpha fade can only take the whole ribbon down together - which, combined with the widening, is the entire reason the old version looked like a wave rather than a dissolve. The brightness envelope obeys the same rule: the first pass cooled the copy with `exp(-t/0.2s)`, which had it at a fifth of its glow before the mask had eaten half of it - a fade wearing a mask. It now slides only ~30% over the phase (per end, so the slower amber foot cools slower), and the mask alone decides when a wisp dies; a small terminal smoothstep over the last 15% of the phase takes out the few wisps the threshold never quite reaches before the geometry stops drawing.

Three things decide whether the mask reads as wisps or as one wave. **Cell shape**: ribbon space is anisotropic - one v unit is two core widths where one u unit is half of one - and the first mask sampled it raw at `(1.5u, 0.55v)`, stretching every noise cell ~5x down the channel, which is a texture pulled along the whole bolt however good the threshold is. The coordinates are made near-isotropic and dense (`10.4v` against `6u`-equivalent) before any noise reads them, so the cells are small-blob-sized against the thin strip. **Symmetry**: the profile's folded `|across|` must never feed the noise - it mirrors every wisp about the centreline, and the eye picks the symmetry out instantly - so the mask reads the SIGNED across while the profile keeps the fold. **Churn**: at a fixed age the old field was frozen - only the flow's slow roll moved it, at whole-bolt scale. The field now boils like a cloud detail texture: three octaves scrolling against each other at different multiples of the convection direction plus small lateral drifts, so the wisps climb and churn in place at every age. The plasma's across profile also steepens back to the channel's own fourth-power falloff instead of softening with age: the wisps come off the thin core, and the wide gradient around it belongs to the fading glow.

`PlasmaWarp` is the flow amplitude and rides `tangent.z` per vertex rather than a uniform, since only the plasma copies carry it; the noise LOD sits beside it in `tangent.y` and fades the fine octave, because an unresolved octave is only a hash. Nothing here runs above about 8x in strip coordinates, which is what the earlier 40x grain could never survive.

**Flash-boiled water.** A ground strike and its crawl vaporise the standing water they land in. The discs are rolled at spawn beside the fire blobs - the attachment plus one every couple of metres down the crawl, delayed by the same `CRAWL_ARC_M_S` arc so the burst runs outward along the channel - but they are rolled EMPTY. What is under them is a question only contact can answer, ten seconds after the roll, so `vaporiseGround()` runs once as the stroke lands, and each disc takes the water actually beneath it out of `SSSurfaceField` and keeps that figure as its own strength. A strike on a dry road removes nothing and steams not at all; a strike into a flooded street steams down its whole crawl. Once, never per stroke - the second stroke falls on ground the first already boiled dry.

`SSSurfaceField::vaporise()` takes all three media, each weighted by what a return stroke would really do and normalised against a full one of its kind before they are summed: the standing puddle goes outright at the centre, the film of wet mostly goes, the snow loses its top but not the drift, because a bolt does not clear a snowfield. Removal alone is not enough - the next rain tick would put the puddle straight back and the whole effect would read as nothing having happened - so every touched cell also takes a `mScorch` hold, in proportion to its distance across the burst, during which it accumulates no wet, no pool and no snow while its ordinary drying and draining paths keep running. The rim recovers before the middle. `SteamHold` is the dial, at 6 s by default; long values are deliberately available and deliberately a bad idea, since a storm would then strip every puddle it struck near.

This is the one part of the strike that is NOT deterministic across viewers, and cannot be: it reads the live local surface field, whose wetness, puddles and snow are local by construction. It is the same divergence the crawl's wet-steering and every surface height in this file already carry, listed again under Determinism below.

The puff (mode 7) is the only element of the pass that is not a light, so it is the only one drawn alpha-blended, in its own batch after the additive one, with the precipitation pass's separate alpha factors (source `ZERO`, destination `ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA`) so a cloud standing in front of the channel takes the bolt's bloom seed down with it rather than glowing through. An additive white puff over a night storm reads as a second flash, which is exactly wrong. It snaps to width over `STEAM_BURST_S`, then rises and thins over `STEAM_LIFE_S`, broken up by two slowly turning octaves and eaten from the rim with age so it tatters rather than fading whole. Its colour is mixed toward the bolt's own glow while the channel is still lit - the moment that carries the effect - and settles to a cool ambient grey afterwards; that grey is a constant rather than a sampled sky colour, which is the thing to revisit if it reads too bright at night.

**Retirement** waits for the longest of the plasma tail, the fire tail (`3.5 tau_f x 1.4`) and the last spark plus its secondaries, so nothing is cut off mid-air; the occlusion query name is released there and in `clear()`.

## Performance

The depth copy: `SSVolCloud::ensureSceneDepthCopy()` is the puff pass's copy hoisted out and stamped by frame and viewport, taken by whichever pass asks first. Post-deferred passes never write depth, so one copy per frame is exact for both. The lightning pass asks only with `SoftDepth > 0`, a ground show on screen within 400 m and not hidden - a clear sky pays the blit only for those seconds. It must be called before the pass binds its own program (the copy binds `gCopyDepthProgram` and rebinds the screen target). It binds under the reserved `depthMap` name, `ss_soft_on` gates every read (the flash pass runs before any copy exists), and `ss_clip` passes the projection's CONSTANT far plane. Observation, not changed here: `ssvolcloud.cpp` passes `camera->getFar()` (the draw distance) as the far plane for its own linearisation; the puffs' soft distance was tuned by eye against that, so it was left alone.

The occlusion query, kept minimal on request: one `GL_ANY_SAMPLES_PASSED` query per live ground strike within 1.5 km on the ground show's box (foot, crawl, spark landings, fire blobs), the box drawn through the pass's own program in a mode that can never discard, names from the octree's pool, the answer read the next frame without ever waiting, honoured only away from the contact and the restrikes (`mT < -0.3 || since the last stroke > 0.3`), and gating only the ground show (crawl, sparks, fire, aura, swarm) - never the column, the scene light, the flash or the sound. Early-Z already kills occluded fragments; the win is the CPU quad build. Skipped under shader profiling (which holds the one occlusion query GL allows) and below GL 3.3.

Per frame the joint-merge scratch lives on the renderer (no allocations), the joint pass runs only when a copy will draw, crawl nodes skip the puff transmittance walk (never in cloud), and the buffer is capped at 48000 quads.

**Why `drawBatch` unbinds before it binds.** `LLVertexBuffer::setBuffer` only calls `setupVertexBuffer` - the `glVertexAttribPointer` calls that say where this buffer's data lives - when it sees the binding change (`sGLRenderBuffer != mGLBuffer`) or the shader's attribute mask change. A pass that builds and draws in one call defeats both tests at once: `unmapBuffer` binds the buffer to stream the vertex data up and assigns `sGLRenderBuffer` on the way past without configuring one pointer, and the pass's own `bind()` has already set `sLastMask` to its own mask via `setupClientArrays`. `setBuffer` then finds nothing changed and configures nothing, so the draw runs on whatever buffer the previous pass aimed the attributes at - the cloud deck's, or one already handed back to the pool. Symptoms are geometry-shaped rather than shader-shaped: quads counted and segments built while the screen stays empty, occlusion queries returning zero samples on a strike directly in front of the eye (the query box is real geometry, so it is an honest detector that the draw rasterized nothing), and, when the stale pointers happen to address live data, a large wedge of noise where a bolt should be. `LLVertexBuffer::unbind()` immediately before each `setBuffer` zeroes the tracker and forces the full path. The same call precedes the `setBuffer` in `ensureBuffer`, where `LLVBOPool::allocate` creates the mirror-image trap: on a pool miss it has already bound the new name and assigned `sGLRenderBuffer` itself, on a pool hit it binds nothing at all.

The precipitation renderer shares this build-and-draw shape and escapes the fault purely by ordering - it unmaps its buffer before binding its shader, and `bind()`'s own `unbind()` then zeroes the tracker for it. That is worth knowing before treating `sspreciprenderer.cpp` as the pattern to copy: it is correct by accident of sequence, not by construction.

## Determinism, honestly

Lengths, arms, steps, headings, thresholds, spark rolls and fire seeds come from streams seeded by the fire time, so every client agrees on them. The wet-steered heading choice, the crawl's and sparks' surface heights, the fire blobs' heights and the puddle probes read the LOCAL surface field and flowmap capture, so two viewers can differ at ground level by a few metres - the degree of divergence the attachment search already had (its flowmap columns are local too). Chosen over strictly shared geometry because puddles genuinely attracting the crawl was the ask. The flash-boil joins that list and sits furthest along it: which discs steam, and how hard, is read entirely from the local field at contact, so two viewers watching the same strike can see steam in different places and one can see none at all. There is no way around it short of replicating the wetness field, and the effect is worth more than the agreement.

## Dials

`GroundAmber` (strength, existing) and `AmberZone`; `Bead`; `Plasma` and `PlasmaWarp`; `LateRestrike`; `Aura` (ships at 0), `AuraPull`, `SoftDepth`; `HitFlare`; `GroundFire`, `GroundFireLife`, `FireLight`; `Steam`, `SteamHold`; `Crawl`, `CrawlWet`; `SparkSpread`; `OcclusionCull`. All `SSAtmoLightning*`, on the Effects & LOD floater's Lightning tab with the existing rows. Tuning notes: `Glow` (0.4) is the bloom brake - the flare's spike adds up to 0.35 of bloom fraction on its own and the discs accumulate additively; if the scene dims after a near strike, lower `Glow` or `HitFlare`, not the colours.
